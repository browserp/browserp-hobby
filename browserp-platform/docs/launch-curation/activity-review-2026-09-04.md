## Data limits and reproducibility

1. **This is not 30 days of history.** The adapters retain up to 30 days, but this roster was largely imported today. Most histories start around 02:00–02:12 UTC; Cali starts 00:56. No preceding European evening or previous week is in the database. The newest listing has only minutes.
2. Snapshot writes require validated nonnegative integer counts and source observation timestamps. Failed/stale upstream requests set source error state without adding a fabricated zero snapshot; genuine successful zeros remain valid data. Therefore sample counts and medians exclude unsuccessful attempts. They are not uptime metrics or time-weighted averages.
3. Unchanged Cfx source timestamps are deduplicated. Hundreds of sample readings are not hundreds of independent sessions or days. Five-minute public freshness and source errors are separate from this historical threshold decision.
4. Region comes from reviewed listing metadata. US and Australia are broad, so both coastal timezone bounds are shown. “International” and “Not confirmed” receive no invented local evening. Server geography, server name and language were not used to manufacture a timezone.
5. Snapshot history has gaps. No hourly bin is presented as continuous coverage, and no unsupported past peak is inferred in a gap. A higher historical value can retain a low current listing; absence of one in a short window does not prove permanent low activity.
6. Only read-only SELECTs were executed. Root owns any visible archive actions. No schema, listing, source or scheduler data was changed; no detail-route or manual-source warming was done.
